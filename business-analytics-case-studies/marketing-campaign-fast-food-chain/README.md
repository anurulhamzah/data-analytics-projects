# Marketing Campaign on Fast Food Chain Company

A/B-style analysis in a spreadsheet of three promotions for a new menu item, to find which promotion has the greatest effect on sales.

**Tools and methods:** Spreadsheet, XLMiner Analysis ToolPak, descriptive statistics, correlation, ANOVA, A/B testing

## Business context
A fast-food chain plans to add a new item to its menu but has not decided between three marketing campaigns. The item was launched at one location in each of several randomly selected markets, with a different promotion at each location, and weekly sales were recorded for the first four weeks.

## Objectives
- Understand the distribution of store age and sales.
- See how sales vary by market segment, market size, location, store age, promotion, and week.
- Check whether results come from the campaign itself or from other factors such as market segment.
- Make recommendations.

## Key insights
- Promotion 3 gives the highest sales overall.
- Large and medium markets sell the most with promotion 3, while small markets sell the most with promotion 1.
- Hypothesis tests (p-value below 0.05) show the promotions differ in effect. Promotion 1 has the best average result in small and medium markets, and promotion 3 in large markets.
- Market size has a positive correlation with sales: larger markets sell more.

## Recommendations
- Choose the promotion by market size: promotion 1 for small and medium markets, promotion 3 for large markets.
- Investigate why many stores operate for only a short period.
- ANOVA results show that market size affects the sales produced by each promotion, so market size should be controlled for when judging campaigns.
