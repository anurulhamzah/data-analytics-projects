# Divvy Bike Sharing Analysis

Analysis of Chicago's Divvy bike share data with Python and Tableau to develop strategies that increase annual revenue, plus a dashboard of key metrics for tracking company performance.

**Tools and methods:** Python, Tableau, time series analysis, root cause analysis, dashboard

## Business context
Divvy is the bike share system serving Chicago and Evanston. Bikes can be unlocked at one station and returned to any other. The main source of revenue is rental fees, and users pay for ride duration depending on their membership plan.

## Problem statement
- Ride counts and ride duration are low in Q1, rise in Q2 and Q3, and fall again in Q4.
- Ride volume grows over time, yet total daily revenue is decreasing.

## Key insights
- 16,135,915 rides between 2020 and May 2023 (22% in 2020, 34% in 2021, 34% in 2022, 10% in 2023).
- Members take the most rides, but casual users have the longest average ride time and generate more revenue per trip.
- Classic bikes are used most, but electric bike usage is growing and electric bikes generate more revenue than classic bikes from 2022.
- Popular stations: Streeter & Grand Ave, University Ave, Kingsbury St, and Wells St.
- Most rides are shorter than 15 minutes, which points to commuting rather than recreation.
- Usage is lowest in winter and peaks in summer.

## Recommendations
- Promote bike sharing to sustain the upward usage trend, for example community biking events with bike communities, or health and environment content.
- Expand capacity at the highest-demand stations by moving bikes from low-usage locations.
- Run marketing campaigns and incentives in summer, when usage peaks.
- Monitor electric bike usage and increase their availability, for example by replacing missing or broken bikes with electric ones.
- Make sure docking stations are well located and working, to support short commuting rides.
